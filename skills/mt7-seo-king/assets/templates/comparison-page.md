# Comparison page brief ("<A> vs <B>", "<X> alternatives", "<option 1> or <option 2>?")

`[PROVEN]` "[A] vs [B]" and "[tool] alternatives" are winnable bottom-of-funnel patterns. `[DATA]` "best X" lists are 43.8% of ChatGPT-cited page types ([Ahrefs, 2025-12](https://ahrefs.com/blog/best-lists-research/)) - but `[WARNED]` self-ranking listicles were tied to 29-49% visibility drops and AI Overviews omitted self-crowning publishers 83% of the time (contested). The line: compare honestly, name real alternatives, never crown yourself.

## Gate
- [ ] You have first-hand experience with every option compared (used it, built with it, migrated from it).
- [ ] The comparison would be useful even to someone who does not buy from you.
- [ ] The page does not rank the publisher #1 or hide the competitors' strengths.

## Pick the format (they serve different searchers) `[PROVEN]` `[INFERENCE]`
- "<X> alternative" (singular): someone leaving X. Lead with why people switch and the closest like-for-like option.
- "<X> alternatives" (plural): someone shortlisting. 4-7 real options, the selection criteria up front, each option's best-for.
- "<You> vs <X>": a buyer deciding between two. Your case belongs in the labelled "where you fit" section, after the neutral comparison.
- "<A> vs <B>" (neither is you): the most trusted format; you appear, if at all, as a clearly labelled third option.

## Evidence discipline (adapted from marketing-skills `competitors`, MIT)
- Date every competitor fact and link its source; a single visit is a snapshot.
- "Not observed" is not "doesn't have": write "not listed on their pricing page (checked <date>)".
- Keep three layers apart: fact (what the page says), interpretation (what it means for the reader), recommendation (who should pick what).
- Keep one facts file per competitor and re-check it before every update of the page.

## Fill-in
- Options compared: ...  Reader's decision: ...
- Primary query: ...  Related: "<A> pricing", "<A> vs <B> for <use case>", "is <A> worth it"
- URL: /compare/<a-vs-b>  Title in the searcher's words: ...  Meta description: ...  h1: ...

## Skeleton
1. **Verdict first:** who should pick which, in 3-5 sentences (the AI-quotable passage).
2. **Comparison table** - criteria buyers use (cost, time to ship, ownership, maintenance, lock-in, support, performance); real numbers with dates and sources.
3. **Per-option sections:** strengths, weaknesses, best for, what it costs (verified, dated).
4. **How we chose the criteria** (methodology; what you tested; disclosure of your own interest).
5. **Where you fit** - one clearly labelled section, after the neutral analysis, with the honest case for and against yourself.
6. **Buyer questions** in prose.
7. **Links** to service pages / case studies; CTA.

## Technical
Article JSON-LD with real author; no AggregateRating/Review about yourself `[G]`; update dates only when the comparison substantively changes `[G]`.

## Do not
- Publish "Top 10 <category> companies in <city>" with yourself at #1 `[WARNED]`; pitch independent list publishers instead (`assets/outreach-kit.md`).
- Invent competitor pricing or quote figures without a date.
- Generate a comparison per competitor at scale from a template `[G]`.
